Brake Thermal Dynamics Lab

Real-world braking physics, energy conversion, and rotor temperature analysis for Baja / Formula Student vehicles

Every emergency stop converts your vehicle's kinetic energy into heat at the pad–rotor interface. This lab quantifies that conversion and predicts rotor temperatures under realistic braking scenarios. Use the Calculator to input your vehicle parameters and get instant results with graphs and Excel export, or jump to the Simulation for a live thermal and hydraulic model with interactive brake control.

Disc Brake Assembly (Front Axle Representative)

Rotor Cast iron or steel; Ø ≈ 330 mm; cross-drilled for cooling.
Caliper Floating design; single 25.4 mm piston; aluminum body.
Brake Pads Organic or semi-metallic; swept area ≈ 47 cm² per pad.
Brake Line Flexible hose from master cylinder; DOT 3/4 fluid.

Physics and Thermal Equations

Kinetic Energy (with rotational overhead)

E = ½ × M × v² × k

k ≈ 1.15 accounts for rotating un-sprung mass (wheels, hubs, rotors).

Energy Split (brake bias)

E_front = E × 0.75 E_disc = E_axle ÷ 2

Weight transfer biases braking front-heavy. Two discs per axle.

Stopping Performance

a = μ × g t_stop = v ÷ a

Traction-limited deceleration. g = 9.80665 m/s².

Braking Power and Flux

P = E ÷ t q = P ÷ A_rubbing

Flux (W/m²) drives pad fade and rotor thermal cracking.

Temperature Rise (peak surface)

ΔT_peak = E ÷ (m_surface × c_p)

Uses action-surface mass only for instantaneous peak temperature.

Settled Temperature (soak)

ΔT_soak = E ÷ (m_full × c_p)

Full rotor mass gives stable long-term equilibrium temperature.
